## It Is Genuinely, Irritatingly Good

I want to be clear before the complaints start: the writing here is better than it has any commercial reason to be. Whole story arcs land with a confidence most full-price RPGs never reach, the soundtrack is doing real work, and the turn-based combat is legible and sharp in a way that respects the fact you might be playing it on a phone on a train.

That is exactly the problem. If it were bad, I would have left.

## The Treadmill

This is a game engineered so that you can never finish it, and every system reflects that. Energy regenerates on a clock, so the game decides how much of it you are allowed to play today. Story arrives on a version cadence, so the ending is always one patch away, permanently. And the character economy runs on odds — you do not earn the thing you want, you roll for it, and the game is very careful never to phrase it that way.

> It is a story I love, distributed on a schedule designed by people who have studied exactly how long I will keep coming back.

Then power creep quietly invalidates the team you spent months building, and the endgame becomes a spreadsheet where the answer is a unit released after the problem. None of this is a bug. All of it is the business model, wearing the costume of a JRPG.

## Why I'm Still Logging In

Because the characters got me, and the writers knew they would. That is not a defense of the structure — it is the mechanism. The affection is real and it is also the hook, and being able to see the hook clearly has never once stopped me from biting.

More hours here than in anything else I own. That is not a recommendation. It is the complaint, stated numerically. If this were a sixty-dollar box with an ending, it would be one of my favorite games of the decade, and I would have my evenings back.
